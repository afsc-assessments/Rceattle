Detailed Description

One-step-ahead (OSA) residual helpers for composition data.

TMB's oneStepPredict() needs every observation in a flat obsvec, its likelihood term multiplied by its own keep element, which multivariate discrete composition data cannot supply directly. Following Trijoulet et al. (2023) and WHAM (src/age_comp_osa.hpp), a composition of A bins is decomposed into A-1 univariate conditionals – binomials for the multinomial, beta-binomials for the Dirichlet-multinomial – each gated by its keep element. The last bin is fixed by the sum-to-N constraint, contributes nothing, and is reported as NA on the R side. With every keep at 1 the conditional log-densities sum to the joint log-density, so OSA changes how the likelihood is decomposed, not its value. The _osa suffix and the data_indicator argument keep these clear of TMB's dmultinom() and of the ddirmultinom() in helper_functions.hpp that ordinary fitting uses.

The file also holds the conditional CDFs oneStepPredict(method = "cdf") reads – continuous binomial for a composition bin, normal / left-truncated normal for an aggregate series. That residual is Phi^-1(F(x)), formed without a conditional mean, so it cannot leave the support of a bounded observation.

Shared contract of the CDF terms below – the two _cdf_terms helpers and dmultinom_osa()'s do_cdf path. Each contributes to the log-likelihood keep.cdf_lower times log F plus keep.cdf_upper times log of its complement, which is 0 whenever the cdf gates are 0: every evaluation except the two oneStepPredict(method = "cdf") makes per observation. The terms are added only when the caller asks (do_cdf, from osa_mode == 2), so ordinary fitting and the Gaussian OSA methods are unchanged and pay nothing for them. Every F is mapped onto the open interval by osa_squeeze_cdf() first, because an exactly 0 or 1 CDF puts a -Inf on the tape and a -Inf times a zero gate is a NaN.

Keep-aware Dirichlet-multinomial log-density via conditional beta-binomials.

No do_cdf counterpart: a beta-binomial has no elementary CDF, and the reference route – summing the pmf over 0..floor(x) (contrib/OSA_multivariate_dists-main/distr.hpp, pbetabinom) – is a step function of a fractional count costing O(x) beta functions per bin, thousands of times the multinomial path at CEATTLE sample sizes. So a Dirichlet-multinomial fleet cannot be residualized with oneStepPredict(method = "cdf"); osa_residuals() sends it to a Gaussian method instead.

One-step-ahead CDF terms for a normal observation.

Every aggregate series in CEATTLE is normal on the scale obsvec stores it in – log for lognormal catch and index, natural scale for a "Normal" index, the whitened L^-1 scale for "MVN" – so its conditional CDF is pnorm of the standardized residual.

Conditional binomial CDF P(X <= x), X ~ Binomial(n, p), at fractional x.

Written through the regularized incomplete beta, 1 - I_p(x + 1, n - x), which equals the binomial CDF at whole counts and stays smooth at the fractional ones composition data carry: a bin count is proportion * effective sample size, an integer only by coincidence. Same expression as the Trijoulet et al. (2023) reference implementation TMB ships (contrib/OSA_multivariate_dists-main/distr.hpp, dists::pbinom).

◆ osa_squeeze_cdf()

template<class Type>
Type osa_squeeze_cdf ( Type u)

Map a CDF onto the open interval before its logarithm is taken.

Four machine epsilons wide where osa_squeeze() uses one: oneStepPredict recovers F from 1/(1 + exp(nlcdf.lower - nlcdf.upper)) in double precision, and at one epsilon that denominator sits on the round-half-to-even tie above 1 – where it rounds to 1 so does F, and the residual is infinite. Measured on BS2017SS: 6 of 4538 composition residuals infinite at one epsilon, none at four; the cost is where |residual| saturates – 8.04 against 8.13 – a tail no Q-Q plot resolves.

It maps rather than clamps, so a caller that forms F by subtracting two CDFs relies on it to absorb the cancellation: osa_trunc_norm_cdf_terms()'s numerator can round to about -2e-16 at an observation near zero, which this shift carries back above 0 before the log. Narrowing it would return NaN there.

Type mu,

One-step-ahead CDF terms for a normal left-truncated at zero.

With z = (x - mu)/sd and m = mu/sd, the truncated CDF is F(x) = [Phi(z) - Phi(-m)] / Phi(m) and its complement is 1 - F(x) = Phi(-z) / Phi(m), because Phi(m) + Phi(-m) = 1. Both are written in that form rather than as 1 - F, so the upper tail keeps its precision. This is exact wherever the truncation carries mass, which is what the Gaussian OSA methods cannot see: the truncation enters the density only through log Phi(m), a function of the prediction and not of the observation.

xObservation (natural scale, > 0).
muPredicted index (natural scale).
