Population, SSB and fleet weight-at-age (kg) for every species and fleet.

Empirical weight-at-age (growth_model == 0) is read straight from weight_obs, each projection year carrying the last hindcast year forward. Under estimated growth (growth_model > 0) population weight comes from estimate_growth() at Jan-1, and SSB and fleet weight from estimate_growth_within_yr() at the spawning month and at each fleet's own month. Arguments not listed below pass through to those two, except n_flt, which this function's own fleet loop consumes.

Jan-1 (month 0) mean length, age-length key and weight-at-age for one species.

Mean length-at-age (cm) is anchored at \(L(a_{L1}) = l_1\):

  • von Bertalanffy (growth_model 1): \(L_a = L_{\infty} + (l_1 - L_{\infty}) \cdot e^{-K(a - a_{L1})}\)
  • Richards (2): \(L_a = [L_{\infty}^m + (l_1^m - L_{\infty}^m) \cdot e^{-K(a - a_{L1})}]^{1/m}\)
  • Below the anchor, a linear ramp from Lmin_sp at age 0 to \(l_1\) at age_L1.
  • After year 0 a cohort recursion advances \(L_{a, y}\) from \(L_{a-1, y-1}\) with the previous year's parameters; where that predecessor is still on the ramp, or at slot 0, the closed-form anchor at \(l_1\) is used instead.

Weight-at-age (kg) is integrated across the length distribution rather than taken at mean length, which Jensen's inequality would bias: \(W_a = \sum_{ln} P(ln | a) \cdot \alpha \cdot L_{mid}^{\beta}\).

Mean length-at-age, age-length key and weight-at-age fracyr into the year.

Advances the Jan-1 mean length stored at id_pop by fracyr of growth, for SSB weight-at-age at the spawning month and each fleet's weight-at-age at its own month:

  • von Bertalanffy (growth_model 1): \(L_a = L_{\infty} + (L_{a, jan1} - L_{\infty}) \cdot e^{-K \cdot fracyr}\)
  • Richards (2): \(L_a = [L_{\infty}^m + (L_{a, jan1}^m - L_{\infty}^m) \cdot e^{-K \cdot fracyr}]^{1/m}\)
  • Below the anchor age (current_age <= age_L1) the linear ramp applies, as at Jan-1.

Under growth_plus_length == 1 the plus group keeps the Jan-1 mean length estimate_growth() set for it; under the SS3 forms (2-4) it grows within the year like every other age, as SS3 does.

Weight-at-age (kg) is integrated over the length distribution as in estimate_growth().

Age-length key, weight-at-age and mature weight-at-age for one cell.

Length is normal with mean mu and SD sd (cm) on the population length bins lengths_pop (lower edges): the first bin is a minus group, the last a plus group (SS3's convention).

Weight-at-age is \(\sum_l P(l|a) \alpha L_{mid}^\beta\) at population-bin midpoints (kg). With maturity-at-length, mature weight-at-age is the same sum with the logistic maturity \(1/(1+e^{-s(L_{mid} - L_{50})})\) inside: SS3's fecundity-at-age when fecundity equals body weight.

SD of length-at-age (cm) for one age, year and sex.

The variability term v is \(e^{sd_0}\) at or below age_L1, pinned to \(e^{sd_1}\) for the plus group under sd_style == 1 (WHAM; also SS3 with Growth_Age_for_L2 = 999), and otherwise interpolated linearly in length between \((l_1, e^{sd_0})\) and \((L_\infty, e^{sd_1})\). sd_form 1 makes v an SD in cm (SS3 CV_Growth_Pattern 2), 2 a CV, so SD = v * L (SS3 CV_Growth_Pattern 0).
